# An Onboarding Path That Replaces Your Wiki's Left Sidebar

Every engineering team has the same wiki problem: it's *complete* and it's *unusable*. Forty top-level pages, half updated in 2024, a left sidebar that doubles as a graveyard. New hires are told "it's all in the wiki" and left to wander.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: documentation completeness and onboarding are different products. The wiki is an archive — everything, in no particular order, for readers who already know what they're looking for. Onboarding is a **sequence** — a small, ordered slice for someone who doesn't know anything yet.

The fix isn't a better wiki. It's a path.

## What week one should actually contain

A good developer onboarding path is short enough to finish and ordered so each step unlocks the next:

### Day one: access
The unglamorous stuff, in order: accounts, repos, CI access, the three channels that matter, the password manager, the deploy dashboard. One link each. Yes, it reads like a checklist — that's the point. Nothing derails week one like discovering at 4pm that you can't merge.

### Day two to three: the first real change
Pick a ticket sized for a stranger: fix a typo in the docs, add a missing test. Then link the sequence: where the repo lives, how to run the test suite locally, the branch naming thing, what "LGTM" actually means on your team, how deploys ship. The goal isn't the ticket. It's that by Wednesday, your new hire has pushed code and watched it go to production.

### The rest of week one: how we work
The stuff that's tribal by definition and therefore exists nowhere in writing: how code review actually goes (response times, tone, when to push back), how incidents work, what the weekly meetings are and which are skippable, who owns what. Link to people, not just documents — "ask Priya about the payments service" beats a 14-page service doc written in 2022.

### The map, not the territory
End with the five links a new hire will *revisit*: the architecture overview, the API conventions, the runbook index, the team roster with areas of ownership, and — importantly — a way to give feedback on the path itself.

## Design rules that keep it alive

- **One owner per section.** Not a committee. When the deploy process changes, one person updates one section.
- **Link before you write.** Prefer the existing doc, however rough, over a duplicate. Only write new pages where nothing exists — and then the path points at them.
- **Keep week one the constraint.** If the path can't be finished in a week, cut it. "We'll get to it in onboarding week two" is where paths go to die.
- **Feedback is part of the path.** The last link should make it trivial to say "section 3 assumed I knew Kubernetes." Every new hire is a free audit of your documentation.

## The payoff

The senior engineer who used to spend a day per hire on "let me walk you through the setup" writes the path once and reviews it quarterly. New hires get a consistent week one instead of whichever week one their onboarding buddy happened to improvise. And the wiki stays what it should be — an archive the path can point into, not a maze people are lost in.
